## Required review before treating the policy as final

The owner should obtain qualified legal review and record the outcome before a production or commercial launch, especially for:

1. **Ownership and authority:** confirm who owns or is authorized to license every existing project file, including scripts, documentation and future code; resolve employment, contractor, joint-authorship or prior-assignment issues.
2. **Inbound contributions:** approve a contributor agreement or DCO policy (or an explicitly reviewed alternative) before accepting substantial external code contributions. The current repository does not claim that a CLA or DCO is in force.
3. **Third-party provenance:** audit every dependency, copied snippet, generated artifact, workflow action and bundled asset; preserve required notices and verify license compatibility.
4. **Patent and secondary-license implications:** review MPL-2.0 patent grants, termination provisions, and the Section 3.3/10.4 secondary-license mechanism in the context of the Community/Enterprise boundary.
5. **Commercial and jurisdictional terms:** review distribution, export/sanctions obligations where relevant, trademark policy, governing-law implications and any local mandatory rules.
6. **Edition separation:** verify that no Enterprise code, confidential design, proprietary dependency or internal documentation is present in Community history or release artifacts.

## Change control

- Do not replace MPL-2.0 with a custom or modified version of the license text.
- Any change of license, dual-licensing plan, or change to the covered scope requires a separate owner-approved decision and legal review.
- Keep Enterprise proprietary licensing documented only in the private Enterprise repository.
- Record the final legal decision and effective scope in a follow-up ADR before describing the Community license as legally finalized.
